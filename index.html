Features

Strong encryption, made simple

Every task is a few clear steps, and every result is checked before it is kept. Nothing is left to chance — or to your memory.

Encrypt files

Any file becomes name.crypten, with SHA3-512 hashes of the original and of the encrypted file saved beside it. Keeping the original is your choice.

Encrypt folders

A whole folder is compressed into one encrypted file — then decrypted again, and every file compared with its original.

Encrypted workspaces

Open one, work on its files with your usual programs, save or close it. Every change is a new version, and the versions before are kept until you delete them.

Decrypt or verify

Get a file or a folder back under a free name — nothing is overwritten — or only check that an encrypted file is intact.

Keys that are yours

256-bit keys, made on your computer, each saved in a key file locked with your password. Name them your way and keep a copy on a USB stick.

Passwords you can change

A new password re-encrypts the key file. The key itself stays the same, so everything it encrypted stays readable.

Guided, step by step

Every task is a short wizard whose Next waits until the step is right. Not sure where to start? Guide me step by step asks what you want to do.

Windows and Linux

One app and one set of formats: a folder encrypted on one system opens on the other. Light and dark themes follow your system.

Security

Built so that nothing gets lost

Strong encryption is only half the job. The other half is never harming what you encrypt: Crypten never works on your original, checks every result before it keeps it, and comes through a crash at any moment.

  1. 1Copy

    Your original is copied into a hidden work folder. It is never worked on.

  2. 2Encrypt

    AES-256-GCM, in authenticated 1 MiB segments, with a fresh subkey for every file.

  3. 3Decrypt again

    The result is decrypted once more, straight away.

  4. 4Compare

    Its content must match the original's SHA3-512 hash — for a folder, file by file.

  5. 5Keep

    Only then is it renamed into place. Nothing is ever overwritten.

Crash-safe at every step

A power cut, a blue screen or a killed process at any moment leaves either the state before the operation or the state after it — never a half-written file under a real name, never a lost original. Interrupted work is finished or rolled back the next time Crypten starts, and every step of every operation is tested with a simulated crash.

Ready for tomorrow's cryptography

Every key is made for one cryptography version, recorded in its key file and in every file it encrypts — today AES-256-GCM, CV1. A stronger algorithm can arrive as CV2 without touching anything you have already encrypted.

No one from Crypten — not our developers, not our “company” (we are not classified and operated as a company), truly no one — will ever ask you for your password, your key files or any of your data. Not by email, not in a message, not on the phone, not for any reason.

  • Beware of fake emails. Scammers may copy our name and our look. Write only to our official address, obi.tirips@gmail.com, and always check who a message really comes from before you answer it, open its links or download what it sends.
  • Lost means lost. If you forget your password or lose your key file, we cannot recover them — and neither can anyone else. That is not a flaw: strong encryption is built so that it can never be broken or reversed.

Before your first start

Crypten says it at every start, and we say it here: nothing can be recovered without both the key file and its password. Keep a copy of your key file somewhere safe — a USB stick will do — and never forget its password.

Check your download

Every file here is signed with Crypten's own ML-DSA-87 key, and its signature and its SHA3-512 hash are shown beside it (open Signature and SHA3-512 under its download). With Crypten already on your computer, open About → Signatures → Check a download, choose the file and paste its signature. The first time, compare the key fingerprint About shows with this one (the public key is here too):

f94b4e9b 92a88a5b cbe98be9 71c2065e 953d0828 95183af2 031d74df 5cc468b9 7b0058aa 8c0a6e6c 88f2d1f8 0304ba31 dd6d8ac8 f465b310 d34d8f9f 04a35b1a

Then check the file's SHA3-512 with a second tool as well, one that is not Crypten: a copy of Crypten that was tampered with could call any file genuine. Free tools that compute it, <file> being the file you downloaded:

  • Windows 11certutil -hashfile <file> SHA3-512
  • Linuxopenssl dgst -sha3-512 <file>
  • Python 3python3 -c "import hashlib,sys; print(hashlib.sha3_512(open(sys.argv[1],'rb').read()).hexdigest())" <file>
  • Git Bashopenssl dgst -sha3-512 <file>

certutil is built into Windows 11 24H2 and newer. Python 3 works on every system (type py instead of python3 on Windows), and Git Bash, from Git for Windows, on any Windows.

Before you update, keep the version you have

Every new version of Crypten is made to open what earlier versions encrypted — but keep the version you use now until you are sure. Install the new one, check that it verifies and decrypts your existing encrypted files and workspaces, and only then remove the old one.

Earlier versions are, in most cases, still in the download history of Crypten's releases on GitHub — but your own copy of the runner you use now is the safer one. On Android, Google Play installs updates on its own: to keep your version until you have checked a new one, open Crypten in Google Play, tap the three-dot menu and untick Enable auto update. The desktop app reads the very same files.

If a new version cannot open your data

Every new version of Crypten is made to open what earlier versions encrypted. If one ever cannot — it says a key, a file, a folder or a workspace needs another version, or will not open it — nothing is lost: your data is still encrypted and intact. Get it out with the version that made it, then encrypt it again with the new one.

  1. Get the older version — the one you used last. Your own copy is the safest; earlier versions are, in most cases, in the download history of Crypten's releases on GitHub (github.com/obi-tirips/crypten/releases).
  2. In the older version, decrypt the files and folders you need, and export decrypted copies of your workspaces (Export decrypted copy, on each workspace or any of its versions).
  3. Save that decrypted data in a place only you can reach. It is not encrypted while it waits there, so keep that time short.
  4. Open the newer version and encrypt the data again — with the same key if the newer version accepts it, otherwise with a new key you create in it (and keep that key file and its password safe, as always).
  5. Check that the new encrypted copies verify and decrypt, then delete the decrypted data.

Which version made it: a key file names the app and version that created it in its Created-By line, and a workspace in its crypten-workspace.properties (createdBy, and sealedBy for its last save).

Google Play offers only its newest version, so an older Android app cannot be installed from it again. If it has already updated: back up the workspace (Back up), export its key file (Key & Security, Export), copy them and your encrypted files to a computer, and follow these steps there with the desktop app of the version that made them — it reads the Android app's keys, files and workspaces.

Both apps show these steps under Recovery: the desktop app in its side menu, the Android app in Settings and About.

Questions

Good questions, straight answers

Is Crypten really free?

Yes. Crypten is free to use, for everyone, and it will stay free — forever. There is no account, no subscription and no advertising. Donations keep its development going, but they are never required.

What if I forget my password or lose my key file?

Then what that key encrypted cannot be opened again — not by you, not by us, not by anyone. That is what strong encryption is for, and Crypten reminds you of it at every start. Keep a copy of your key file somewhere safe, a USB stick for example, and never forget its password.

Does Crypten send my files or keys anywhere?

No. Crypten works on your computer and sends nothing anywhere: no cloud, no account, no telemetry. Your files, your keys and your passwords stay with you.

What if my computer crashes while Crypten is working?

Nothing is lost. Crypten never works on your original and records every step as it goes, so the next time it starts it finishes the interrupted work or rolls it back. You are left with the state before the operation or the state after it — never a half-written file.

Can I open my files on another computer?

Yes. Crypten on Windows and Linux reads and writes the same formats. Bring the encrypted file and its key file, add the key file to Crypten there, and unlock it with its password.

Which cryptography does Crypten use?

AES-256-GCM — cryptography version CV1, through Bouncy Castle — in authenticated 1 MiB segments, with a fresh HKDF-SHA-512 subkey for every file. Key files are locked with your password through Argon2id, and every result is checked against SHA3-512 hashes. A stronger algorithm can be added later as a new version, without touching anything already encrypted.

What happens if I uninstall Crypten?

On a computer, uninstalling removes only the program: your key files and workspaces stay where you saved them. Before you uninstall, or to move to another device, use Export everything (Settings or About): it copies your key files, workspaces and encrypted files into one folder — a USB drive works — encrypted, as we advise, or unencrypted after a clear warning that anyone who opens that folder can read it. The Android app, still in development, keeps its keys and workspaces in its own storage, which an uninstall deletes: it will keep an encrypted copy in a backup folder you choose and restore from it. Always keep copies of your key files, and keep your passwords yourself — Crypten never stores one.

Will someone from Crypten ever contact me?

Only to answer when you write to us, and only from obi.tirips@gmail.com. No one from Crypten will ever ask for your password, your keys or your data: a message that does is not from us.
